# Jeffy Commerce — Investor Video Script (HeyGen)

**Format:** AI avatar pitch video · **Target length:** ~3 minutes
**Audience:** A single investor / investment committee
**Tone:** Calm, confident, founder-to-founder. Conviction, not hype.
**Wish List mechanic:** submit 1–10 wishes → weekly random draw → wish granted free → celebrated on radio, newspaper & social. (No voting.)
**Focus:** the business model — why and how it works, why it's brilliant. No schools/mission content.

## PASTE THIS INTO HEYGEN

South Africans overpay for almost everything they buy online — not because the goods cost more, but because of everyone standing between the factory and the front door. Jeffy cuts out those middlemen, and puts your neighbour in their place.

Here's how it works. We import directly from China under our own South African importer's code, then wholesale that stock to a network of independent local sellers — one per town, one per suburb. They keep the stock at home and deliver the same day, in the streets they already know. Customers order on jeffy.co.za. Think Takealot — but the person who hands you your parcel lives around the corner, and runs their own business.

This is the part worth looking at closely, because it's what makes Jeffy both scalable and clean. Our sellers aren't employees and aren't agents — they buy stock from us at wholesale and resell at a margin, the same model Avon and Tupperware ran for decades. So there's no payroll, no minimum wage, no fleet, no franchise baggage. And when a customer pays, PayFast splits that payment automatically — our wholesale share to us, the seller's margin to them, in real time. We never touch money that isn't ours. It's attorney-reviewed, and it keeps our tax clean.

Now the engine that makes Jeffy spread. It's called the Wish List. Anyone can go to our site and submit the things they wish they had — anywhere from one to ten — a fridge, a phone, a gift for their child. No purchase, no catch. Every week we draw winners at random and grant their wish — sourced and delivered, free — and we announce every winner on radio, in the newspaper, and across social media. That does two things at once. It hands us a live map of exactly what South Africans want to buy, so we stock demand instead of guessing. And it turns our marketing into a weekly event people actually want to follow — real publicity, real emotion, at a fraction of the cost of ads.

And here's why I love this model. It's asset-light — no stores, no fleet, no payroll. It only buys what customers have already asked for, so there's almost no dead stock. And it markets itself. Every product is priced off its true landed cost — never guesswork — and we prove the numbers on a small batch in one or two areas before we commit real capital, by the container, into the proven winners. The data aims the cannon.

This isn't a pitch on a napkin. The company is registered, the platform is live, the importer's code is approved, and the seller and Wish List systems are built. We're raising to fund inventory and the first seller cohorts — to turn proven demand into a national network. On our plan, the business breaks even within the first year and returns the investment inside roughly two. Back something that's built to pay its own way.

Jeffy. Factory-direct — delivered by your neighbour.
